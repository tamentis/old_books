All twelve books are translated. Every `§` in the source has a counterpart in the translation, except the garbled first copy of §132–168 and the misplaced duplicate of §1081 that stands where §1181 should be.

The translation is my own reading of the archaic Gheg text. It has not been checked against a published translation, so it is a working draft.

## Source files

- The Markdown was converted from `~/Downloads/KANUNI-I-LEKE-DUKAGJINIT.pdf` (106 pages). The `kanuni_i_leke_dukagjinit.pdf` in the project folder is an empty directory, not a file.
- The PDF has a real text layer, so no OCR was run. The text in it is itself a faulty transcription of the 1933 Shkodër edition (published at www.lezha.net).
- The conversion matches the PDF text character for character, except for one rejoined hyphenated word ("kum-/bonë" → "kumbonë" in §153) and the dropped page numbers.
- The cover image from page 1 is saved as `kanuni_i_leke_dukagjinit_cover.jpeg`.

## Conventions

- Headings mirror the Albanian file: Libri → Book, Artikull → Article, Krye → Chapter, Nye → Section. `§` numbers are kept as in the source and bolded.
- Words in [square brackets] are added for clarity, or mark a place where the source is corrupt or unclear.
- Defects in the source are kept and flagged, not silently corrected.
- Paragraphs that are too corrupt to read are marked as such and not guessed at.

Terms left in Albanian because I could not identify them:
- *babune*: a measure of wheat (§45), and of the miller's toll (§387).
- *strufulleca*: a role at the wedding (§46 and §49).
- *koshiq*: a measure of grain, perhaps a small basket (from *kosh*) (§319).
- *perzivoj* and *kariqe*: kinds of fish trap (§437).
- *rrjell*: apparently something used to stun fish (§444–446).
- *huta*: the Martini rifle (§484).
- *kashata*: perhaps a round of cheese or of bread (§601 j, §663–664). In §663 *nyjen* is the verb "cuts", not a second item.
- *bracat* and *biramelat*: apparently minors and the simple-minded (§768, §774–776).
- *hànor* and *vergan*: kinds of favoured animal that may not be raided (§799).
- *Razogj*: the "two meals of bread" owed to the House of Gjomarku (§1142).
- *lima*: something belonging to the House of Gjomarku in house-burnings (§1145).

## Defects in the source PDF

- **Duplicated block:** §132–168 appear twice. The first copy, at the end of Book Three, is badly garbled (e.g. `g«-,,mës ok-e per k-r~e`); the second, at the start of Book Four, is clean. The translation marks the first copy with a note and translates only the clean one.
- **Missing paragraphs:** §88–98 are absent, so the inheritance chapter opens mid-list at §99. §281 is also absent (the source jumps from §280 to §282).
- **Missing section:** Section 37 is absent; the source goes from Section 36 to Section 38.
- **Section number typos:** §65 is printed as a second §64; §80 as `§.8 O.`; §110 as `§.11 O.`; §601 as `§.691.`; §1115 as `§.1415.`. The translation uses the correct numbers and notes the printed ones.
- **Misplaced duplicate:** where §1181 should be, the source repeats §1081 (the informer's shoes) word for word. The translation notes the gap.
- **Duplicated paragraph:** §773 repeats §772 word for word.
- **Typos in numbers:** "më 241 Poronikë" in §592 c, read as 24 jurors; a fold-raid fine of 600 grosh in §789, where 500 is used everywhere else.
- **Missing item:** §1227 jumps from item f to item h.
- **Misplaced chapter headings:** Book Twelve opens with the headings of Chapters Twenty-Three and Twenty-Four one after the other; the paragraphs of Chapter Twenty-Three come first.
- **Probable lost negation:** §959 says the house of a suicide "incurs a fine", but the reason given only makes sense if it does not. Kept as printed, with a note.
- **Misprints read in the translation:** §62 h *fanë* for *t'anë*; the place name "Thkella" for Kthella (in the list of assembly places; elsewhere left as printed).
- **Unexplained note mark:** "(3)" in §533.
- **Gaps in the list of assembly places** before §1113: several districts have only a "?", and the last two entries are numbered "??".
- **Two items numbered 10:** in §20 the source has two consecutive items "10)".
- **Misnumbered sections:** the first four sections of Book Five are printed as Forty-Six to Forty-Nine; by sequence they are Sixty-Six to Sixty-Nine. The translation gives the correct numbers and notes the printed ones.
- **Duplicated paragraph:** §289 repeats §271 almost word for word, and says so ("See Section LXIII").
- **Incomplete paragraph:** §483 breaks off mid-sentence ("by Saint [...]'s day").
- **Garbled cross-reference:** §510 points to "Liber IV, Nye LXXXII, 'Uhaja', £56", but "The Loan" is Section Eighty-Two of Book Six.
- **Truncated paragraph:** §519, the last paragraph of Book Six, breaks off mid-sentence.
- **Suspicious figures in the price list (§484):** an oak "up to 23" grosh, and a pack horse at 590 (likely 500).
- **Run-together items:** in several places two numbered items share one line (e.g. §4 items 3–4, §9 items 4–5, §17 items 4–5); the translation keeps them together.

### Book Nine — Damages (§735–765)

## Review (2026-10-14)

The whole translation was reviewed against the Albanian by five independent reviewers (Books 1–3, 4–5, 6–9, 10, 11–12), who also checked unusual terms online. I checked each proposed fix against the source before applying it. About 70 changes were made; the main ones:

- **Mistranslations corrected:**
  - §266: the guards fail to *pull the axe out*, not to catch the man.
  - §663: the guest *cuts* the *kashata*; there is no "joint".
  - §688: the words are spoken by the surety, not the debtor.
  - §382 and §682: sunrise and sunset were reversed.
  - §1244: the thanks for a meal, not a corpse being laid out.
  - §1049: the court names 12 jurors for the chief.
  - §1194 i: the clan that does not *lead* against the offenders.
  - §1193: "in death and in life".
  - §768 a: *kafshë* is "something", not "an animal".
  - §593 and §891: "for it is God who grants looks".
  - §29 b: a woman kept "over and above the crown".
  - §62 g: belt arms.
  - §62 h: read and translated.
- **Place names:** Mati at Lis (a place, not "the Oak"); Kthella at Perlat; Berdelec [Bordolec].
- **Identified terms:** *pendë*, *vjetulla* (badger), *pash* (fathom), *kelbazë* (liver rot), *shaterkazi* (hoe and pick, tentative).
- **Consistency:**
  - "Banner" is now capitalised throughout (81 lowercase instances).
  - *berr* is rendered "sheep or goat" or "small stock".
  - *krushq miq* is "escorts who are friends [from outside]" throughout Section 24.
  - *hjeksi* is "complicity", *bestar* is "guarantor of the *besa*", *plyrë* is "wound", *cubni* is "theft".
  - The proverb repeated in §271 and §289 now has the same wording in both places.
- **Proposals not taken:**
  - §656: the guest "has the raki taken away"; *hjek rakin* more naturally means "drink the raki", so a note points to §662 instead.
  - Note after §1199: *kasnecija* as "criers" (see the Book Eleven notes).
  - §50: "neither sought nor asked for", a possible reconstruction of the corrupt proverb, kept as an alternative here rather than in the text.
- **Still unresolved after web searches:**
  - *koshiq*, *rrjell*, *perzivoj*, *kariqe*, *mëdheskë*, *pashtrak*, *tbanë*
  - *zhibla*, *gjengj*, *strufulleca*, *babune*, *kathedret*
  - *karmote më hu*, *mangore*, *bracat/biramelat*, *hànor/vergan*, *um*, *lasin*
  - *zabuka*, *kortarët*, *lima*, *Razogj*, *patalok*
  - "kamba e huej, kaptinat t'ona", "Vra e hup", "jakzat e gjurdijavet"
  - the places Molung, Kerbucë, Bendë and Laç Sebaste
- **Copyright check:** one rendering (the Section 29 proverb) turned out to match the widely quoted Fox wording exactly; it has been replaced with a literal rendering. The translation was otherwise made directly from the public-domain Albanian, without consulting Fox.

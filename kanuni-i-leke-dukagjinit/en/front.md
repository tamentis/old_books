# The Kanun of Lekë Dukagjini

### Collected and arranged by Shtjefën Gjeçovi (Shkodër, 1933)

## Introduction {#introduction}

For centuries, the highlanders of northern Albania lived almost without a state or courts. They obeyed a customary law handed down by word of mouth, the Kanun, attributed to Prince Lekë Dukagjini (15th century). The Franciscan Shtjefën Gjeçovi collected it, and his text was published in Shkodër in 1933.

The Kanun governs the whole of life: the Church, the family, marriage, the house, land, livestock, work, the given word, honour, damages and crimes. Authority rests with the Elders, gathered in assembly. Families group into clans, and clans into Banners (*bajrak*), each led by a Standard-bearer.

Three values dominate. The first is honour, which cannot be bought back: "honour taken has no fine" ([§ 597](#p-597)). The second is the *besa*, a sacred word that binds the whole family and can suspend a feud. The third is hospitality: "the house of the Albanian belongs to God and to the guest" ([§ 602](#p-602)). The guest is inviolable, and an offence done to him must be avenged.

From these values comes the blood feud: blood that is shed calls for blood, sometimes over several generations, unless the Elders bring about a reconciliation. Society is strictly patriarchal. A woman leaves her father's house for her husband's and inherits nothing.

## Notes from the web editor {#editor}

The Albanian text is Gjeçovi's, as published in Shkodër in 1933. It comes from a PDF published on www.lezha.net, itself a transcription of that edition with many errors. The Albanian is in the archaic northern (Gheg) spelling of the time, and has not been modernised. Gjeçovi died in 1929, and the text is in the public domain.

**About the translation.** The English translation was made with Claude, an AI model by Anthropic, directly from the Albanian. The whole text was then reviewed against the Albanian a second time, book by book, and about seventy corrections were made. It has not been compared with Leonard Fox's published translation (*The Code of Lekë Dukagjini*, 1989), which is the standard English version and remains under copyright. Treat this one as a careful working draft: some words of the Gheg are still unidentified, and these places are marked.

Conventions:

1. Words in [square brackets] are added for clarity, or mark a place where the source is corrupt or unclear.
2. The divisions follow the original: Book (*Libri*), Article (*Artikull*), Chapter (*Krye*), Section (*Nye*). The paragraphs keep their § numbers, which are the same in every language: a link to `#p-601` points to § 601 in all versions.
3. Gjeçovi's own notes (*Oroe*, *V.O.*) are set apart with a line in the margin.
4. A few customary terms are kept in Albanian (*besa*, *shengjeta*, *sedija*); the others are rendered as listed in the [glossary](#glossary).

**Corrections to the source.** The following errors of the 1933 text or of its transcription have been corrected, in the Albanian and in the translation:

- The first copy of §132–168, badly garbled, at the end of Book Three, has been removed; the clean copy at the start of Book Four is kept.
- Paragraph numbers misprinted: §65 printed as a second §64, §80 as "§.8 O.", §110 as "§.11 O.", §601 as §691, §1115 as §1415.
- Sections Sixty-Six to Sixty-Nine, at the start of Book Five, were printed as Forty-Six to Forty-Nine.
- §773 repeats §772 word for word, and in place of §1181 the source repeats §1081. Both are replaced by a note.
- The place name "Thkella" is read as Kthella.
- The heading of Chapter Twenty-Four (Death) stood directly after that of Chapter Twenty-Three; it has been moved to §1230, where the chapter begins.
- Numbered items printed on a single line have been set out as separate paragraphs.

**Defects left as they are.** These could not be corrected without guessing:

- §88–98 are missing, as is §281, and there is no Section Thirty-Seven.
- §289 repeats §271 almost word for word, and says so.
- §483 and §519 break off mid-sentence.
- §959 says the house of a suicide "incurs a fine", where the reasoning suggests it should not; a negation has probably been lost.
- §1227 jumps from item f to item h, and §20 has two items numbered 10.
- Some figures look wrong: 241 jurors in §592, 600 grosh in §789 where 500 is used everywhere else, a pack horse at 590 grosh in §484.
- The list of assembly places before §1113 has several gaps marked "?".
